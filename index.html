About

First developed internally by former creative director Christos Devaris in 2020, Syracuse Block Condensed is Syracuse University's proprietary display typeface. The font is built to complement the University’s “Sherman” font family—a Goudy serif resurrected by type designer Chester Jenkins in collaboration with Pentagram, with a sans-serif sister—and is based around the geometry of the University’s “Block S” logo (hence the name). Block Condensed brings an amplified graphic expression to our typographic voice while still feeling “at home” alongside other areas of our visual identity.

In 2022, the internal Design team within the Division of Marketing revisited the typeface, an effort led by senior designer Joey Sweener. Taking DeVaris' original work—which included 8 font weights with matching slanted forms—the typeface was slimmed to six weights and optimized for use on the web. This included remastering nearly every glyph, expanding the font's Latin language support and converting it into a variable font. By making it variable, it allows for lightweight distribution of the font family onto the web, and opens up further design extensions into motion.

Built for greatness

Syracuse Block Condensed is a display font, and should be utilized like one. Headlines, groundbreaking announcements, important game-day updates and more are where this font best finds its home. We recommend you keep it out of important places of the user experience, like site navigation and breadcrumbs. Sherman Sans will be a better choice for those uses.

When you use Syracuse Block Condensed at a smaller point size, try to stick to the heavier weights and ensure it's well tracked. As your point size gets larger, you're free to use the thinner weights. Design with dynamism in mind! The goal is to maximize legibility while still being expressive.

Language Support

Syracuse Block Condensed has been designed to support extended Latin characters. This natively provides language support for nearly 100 Latin-based languages, excluding Vietnamese. To provide further language support for non-Latin languages, such as Arabic, Cyrillic, Korean, etc., the Division of Marketing recommends open-source font Oswald as the fallback for those languages. Embed Oswald as a variable font to get all the same functionality as Syracuse Block Condensed.
